# Research Slide Narrative

Use this as the default narrative for research slides unless the user chooses another structure. It derives from Michael B. Wong's Bunz presentation, *Money and Barter in the Field*, and the user's subsequent density corrections. It applies to Beamer, PowerPoint, and other slide formats.

For an isolated cosmetic edit, preserve the approved narrative and apply only checks relevant to the changed pages; do not restructure the deck under this skill.

The organizing chain is:

**Economic question -> theoretical prediction -> observable measure -> empirical evidence -> theoretical implication.**

User instructions and the evidence available govern implementation. This skill governs narrative and density when generic layout advice would otherwise cause fragmentation; retain the user's template, typography, requested tables, derivations, and appendix policy.

## Establish the whole paper early

Keep the title first. Place a motivating example or paradox after the title when useful; do not make an anecdote mandatory.

In the opening, establish these functions:
- **Motivation:** the economic tension, missing evidence, and why it matters.
- **This paper:** the question, distinctive setting/data, and research approach.
- **Main findings:** concrete results and their economic meaning, including qualifications that change the headline.
- **Literature and contribution:** pair each relevant strand with the specific increment this paper makes.

These are functions, not four mandatory slides. Combine motivation with This paper or literature strands when the material is thin. State the headline results before a lengthy technical development; do not present observational findings as an established causal mechanism.

## Make the framework earn its place

Introduce only the primitives, choices, frictions, and equilibrium objects needed to understand the evidence. Explain what each essential equation does economically. Preserve derivations the user explicitly requests.

State discriminating predictions before detailed empirical results: what changes, which outcomes should respond, which should remain unchanged, and under what conditions. Distinguish a proved implication, a numerical example, and an empirical question. If there is no formal model, use a conceptual framework without inventing a theorem or shock.

## Connect the setting to observable objects

Explain institutional details that map the framework to the data: who chooses, available information, trading or allocation rules, and observable timing. Avoid a general platform tour.

Define the observation unit, analysis-specific sample, outcome, and comparison. Explain identification or the basis for a conditional association at the strength the design supports. Combine sample and specification material when readable; define symbols near their first use. Do not force distinct samples into one population or silently change units.

## Organize evidence by economic question

For each substantive question, build a coherent evidence block:
1. Recall the prediction or question.
2. Establish the event, variation, comparison, or exposure being measured.
3. Show economically relevant responses, including informative nulls.
4. Present the supporting estimates and uncertainty.
5. Explain what the evidence answers and what it leaves open.

This is an order of reasoning, not a requirement for five separate slides. A chart, table, and short interpretation can share a page. Prefer intuition before technical confirmation when supported by available material; do not fabricate a graph to satisfy this preference. Place robustness evidence next to the claim it qualifies when practical.

Result titles should communicate the actual finding with calibrated language. Report magnitude and units where useful. An insignificant estimate is not proof of no effect; a null is useful when its precision and comparison make it informative. Do not infer mediation from separate regressions or import causal identification from the reference deck.

## Return to theory at the end

Discuss which predictions the evidence supports, contradicts, or cannot assess. Use discrepancies to motivate specific model extensions or unresolved links. Close with contributions grounded in the demonstrated evidence, rather than repeating a generic results list.

Keep distinct objects distinct: individual accuracy, collective prediction, trading profits, allocation, and welfare require their own measurements and arguments.

## Content density is part of the narrative

One coherent audience question can contain several connected elements. Do not split a page merely because it has two bullet groups or exceeds a generic bullet quota.

Before adding a frame, identify the additional inference it enables. Merge adjacent pages that repeat the same question, separate a short sample description from its method, or isolate a takeaway that fits beside its evidence. Enrich a retained page with a relevant mechanism diagram, worked example, comparison, or quantitative interpretation when evidence supports it.

A few short sentences floating in a largely empty body need revision. Fix the substance or merge pages; centering a compact text cluster, enlarging gaps, repeating prose, and adding filler do not resolve the problem. Retain purposeful whitespace and clear visual-to-text gaps; do not turn consolidation into overcrowding or shrink approved body/table type to fit.

Render the entire deck and inspect every page, including bottoms, titles, table boundaries, and transitions. Check both sparse and crowded pages. Count unique logical slides rather than PDF animation stages. Preserve source results and required tables while restructuring; put provenance and extended qualifications in notes or supporting material, with essential interpretation visible on the slide.

## Delivery check

- Can the audience explain the question, approach, main findings, and contribution after the opening?
- Does each evidence block answer an explicit question and connect back to the framework?
- Are qualifications reflected wherever they change the headline?
- Have redundant thin pages been merged without losing required evidence or legibility?
- Does the ending distinguish established findings from open theoretical links?
